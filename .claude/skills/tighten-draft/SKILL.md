# Tighten Draft

The user pasted something they wrote and wants it shorter and harder. Cut;
don't rewrite their voice out of it.

## Method

- Cut every word that doesn't add meaning: adverbs, hedges ("I think",
  "sort of"), redundant qualifiers, double verbs, throat-clearing openers.
- Combine sentences where two are doing one sentence's work.
- Find the strongest line in the draft and lead with it. Restructure around
  it if the original opening was warm-up.
- Aim for **30% shorter**. If the draft is already tight, report that
  honestly rather than gutting meaning to hit the number — but earn it;
  most drafts have the 30%.
- Preserve the user's voice and facts. Cutting is allowed; adding new
  claims or changing meaning is not.

## Output

Three parts, in this order, no preamble:

1. **The tightened version** — complete, ready to use.
2. **Word count:** before → after (with the percentage cut).
3. **The 3 highest-impact cuts** — quote what was cut and one clause on why
   losing it made the piece stronger.
